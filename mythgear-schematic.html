Tier 0–1 · No electronics required

Spine Strip
Cloth or bamboo along the spine. Marks the body's axis. The cloth itself is the awareness — no sensor needed.
Neck Ring — Bamboo Prosthetic
Lightweight collar: fabric, bamboo, or cord. Extends the proprioceptive field of the head. Amplifies awareness of rotation and tilt without any electronics.
Wrist Ties
Fabric cuffs. May carry bells, seeds, or weighted beads. At Tier 0, sound is the output. The wrist arc becomes audible. The flower painted in air is heard by the room.
Knee Bands
Cloth at the knees. Weighted or textured. The body feels itself moving. A seated person with mobile knees still generates full language.
Sound Output
The garment produces sound — bells, seeds, breath amplified through cloth, a stick against the ground. No screen. No data. The room hears the dance.
Minimum Presence Principle

MythGear must remain fully itself at its least-resourced form.
A body. A surface. An answer.
A breath is enough. A cheek shift is enough. A hand that can move one finger is enough.

Tier 2–4 · Sensor map · 14 points

Crown + Breath
IMU at crown for head rotation and tilt. Pressure sensor at sternum for breath rhythm. Breath becomes spacing in the grammar. A cheek shift is enough.
Shoulders / Elbows / Wrists — bilateral
Six IMU sensors reading the full arm gesture field. Wrist arc becomes punctuation. Elbow extension becomes emphasis. Shoulder rotation marks a phrase boundary.
Spine / Sacrum
Mid-spine for upper body wave. Sacrum for the ground relation. Together they hold the body's vertical syntax. Seated bodies generate full language here.
Knees / Ankles — bilateral
Knee hesitation becomes ellipsis. Ankle trace holds the ground's memory of the step. A person who can only move one ankle is already speaking.
Neck IMU
The bamboo prosthetic neck, now sensor-augmented. Head turn, tilt, micro-rotation. The elder painting a flower — the neck leads the brush.

Local Pattern Field — How the circles are carried

01
Sound or pulse enters
Whale song, drum, ambient field, or silence. Not a command. A question the body may or may not answer.
02
Sensors read the body's answer
All active sensor points stream orientation, acceleration, and pressure locally. No cloud required at Tier 3. The data does not leave the room.
03
The local pattern layer traces rhythm
A lightweight local model reads the joint-sequence stream and identifies grammatical structure: rotation (punctuation), breath-pause (spacing), velocity shift (emphasis), sustained hold (ellipsis), tremor (texture). It does not name what the movement means. It traces the structure of what was said.
04
Translation into perceptible form
The pattern layer outputs a trace: light, sound response, field-change, or mark on a DesertRain surface. The wrist circle becomes a visible circle. The breath pause becomes silence in the output. Pain's ripple becomes a ripple in the field.
05
The body receives the trace
The dancer, elder, or speaker feels seen by the system. Not judged. Not scored. Not assessed. The system said: I noticed that. The body heard: I was perceived.
06
The trace belongs to the body
All output is held locally or released by the body's owner. The pattern layer retains no personal movement profile. The session ends. The trace was the session's.

DesertRain · Receiving Surface

Wrist Arc — The Flower
A wrist circle traced in air becomes a visible circle on the surface. The elder's flower lands. The mark remembers the gesture that made it.
Spine Wave
The wave traveling up the spine becomes a wave in the field. The dancer responding to whale song arrives as sinuous mark on the page.
Breath Spacing
Pause between breaths becomes gap between marks. The page breathes with the body. Silence is not absence — it is punctuation.
Hesitation — Ellipsis
The knee that slows, the shoulder that holds — three dots. Not failure. Grammatical pause. The body already knows this syntax.
Ripple — Pain Over Stone
Pain becomes the ripple over stone. Ecstasy — in motion, soft — becomes water over the selfsame stone. The surface holds the wave's shape without naming what made it.

The Braid — Three surfaces, one grammar

DesertRainWhere marks remember gesture. The page holds the motion of the hand that wrote it.
Translation GarmentWhere gesture becomes mark. The body answers; the garment carries the answer into the visible world.
Embodied LanguageThe grammar they share. Rotation, breath, hesitation, ripple, wave, silence, arc.

DesertRain is not a dashboard. It is not analytics. It is not a record of performance.

It is a surface where the body's speech lands — and rests — without being judged.

The mark belongs to the body that made it. The surface holds no claim on what it received.

MythGear begins wherever a body can answer.
