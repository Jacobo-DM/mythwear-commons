# Body as Lantern

_A MythWear / RacoonLight bridge note_

This note names a possible bridge between **MythWear** and **RacoonLight**.

A child’s movement may guide a field of discovery, but the child must never become the object being measured.

The body may move the light.

The light must not carry data about the body away.

> **The body moves the field.  
> The field does not move data about the body.**

## The Possibility

RacoonLight began as a quiet interface pattern: a soft field of light that reveals what is already present when the reader moves slowly through a dim page.

In MythWear, that movement could become bodily.

A turn of the head, a lean, a pause, or a shift in orientation might guide the light. The screen would no longer respond only to a cursor. It would respond to the body as a participant in discovery.

This opens meaningful possibilities for embodied reading, accessibility, story-world exploration, DiscoveryMode practices, child-led inquiry, gentle attention training, and interfaces that slow the hand and restore curiosity.

But this possibility carries danger from the beginning.

## The Danger

The moment attention can be tracked, attention can be harvested.

The wrong version of this pattern becomes surveillance disguised as wonder.

It becomes gaze tracking.  
It becomes engagement scoring.  
It becomes classroom compliance.  
It becomes behavioral profiling.  
It becomes another way to turn a child’s nervous system into data.

That is not MythWear.

That is capture.

## The Direction of the Relationship

The core distinction is not technical first. It is relational.

In a surveillance system, the body is an input.  
The system receives, records, and interprets.  
The child becomes the object of analysis.

In the commons version, the body is a participant.  
The system responds, but does not remember, evaluate, report, score, or profile.  
The child remains the subject of discovery.

> **Body as lantern, not body as data.**

## Guardrails

Any MythWear / RacoonLight bridge must begin with these constraints:

- local only
- no cloud analytics
- no behavioral profiles
- no hidden recording
- no scoring
- no dashboards
- no compliance monitoring
- no default camera-based gaze tracking
- no precise attention mapping
- no inference of interior states
- child-controlled memory
- visible, opt-in, interruptible interaction

The acceptable version uses gesture, posture, orientation, or proprioceptive movement.

It does not use the body to produce evidence.

A head turn is not a thought.  
A pause is not consent.  
A linger is not a preference profile.

## Classroom Tension

A teacher may legitimately want to know whether a child engaged with something.

That desire can come from care.

But care must not become surveillance.

The likely boundary is this:

> **The child may share the discovery.  
> The system may not report the attention.**

A child can say what they found.  
A child can save a note.  
A child can choose to show a teacher, parent, or friend.

But the system cannot secretly report where the child looked, how long they paused, what drew their attention, or what their body may have indicated.

## Core Law

> **The body moves the field.  
> The field does not move data about the body.**

This is the line that must remain intact.

If it cannot remain intact, the bridge should not be built.
